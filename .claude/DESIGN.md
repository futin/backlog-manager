# Design analysis — the board's visual language

Sections 1–7 below are copied verbatim from ../claude-agents-dashboard/.claude/DESIGN.md at commit c179ca4 (2026-09-12) on 2026-09-15; they describe the reference design framework and not this application. Section 8, to follow, documents how this app applies them.

## 1. Typography

### Typeface
A single geometric-grotesque sans is used for everything — UI, labels and numerals. Characteristics visible in the screenshots: tall x-height, closed apertures, straight-cut terminals, circular `o`/`e`, single-story `a`, flat-sided `$`, tabular-feeling lining numerals, slightly condensed caps in the wordmark.

A geometric grotesque in the vein of the Aeonik / Neue Montreal class. Suggested substitutes, in preference order:

```
font-family: "General Sans", "Hanken Grotesk", "Plus Jakarta Sans", Helvetica, sans-serif;
```

No secondary/display face, no serif, no monospace. Numerals are set in the same face as text (not a separate mono) — set `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` on all figures and table columns.

### Weights in use
| Weight | Where |
|---|---|
| 400 Regular | body copy, secondary labels, table cells, axis labels |
| 500 Medium | nav items, buttons, card titles, legend labels, tab/chip labels |
| 600–700 Semibold/Bold | big money figures, wordmark, percentages in lists |

Only three weights; nothing lighter than 400, nothing heavier than 700.

### Type scale
| Role | Size | Weight | Line height | Tracking |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hero metric (`$12,450`) | 44 px | 700 | 1.05 | −0.02em |
| Large metric (`$15,000`, `$8,450`, `$15,780`) | 30 px | 700 | 1.1 | −0.02em |
| Gauge value (`75%`) | 28 px | 700 | 1.1 | −0.02em |
| Card title (`Monthly spending limit`, `Cost analysis`, `My card`) | 19–20 px | 500 | 1.3 | −0.01em |
| Wordmark | 20 px | 700 | 1 | +0.01em |
| Nav item | 16 px | 500 | 1.4 | 0 |
| Promo heading (`Upgrade to Pro!`) | 20 px | 700 | 1.25 | −0.01em |
| Body / list row (`Housing`, transaction name) | 14–15 px | 400 | 1.45 | 0 |
| Card subtitle (`Balance overview`, `Spending overview`) | 13 px | 400 | 1.4 | 0 |
| Legend, chip label (`7d`, `January`), button label | 13 px | 500 | 1.3 | 0 |
| Meta / axis / tooltip body / date under transaction | 12 px | 400 | 1.35 | 0 |
| Status micro-label (`Completed`, `Declined`), sub-caption (`Left to save 4 months`) | 11 px | 400 | 1.3 | 0 |
| Badge count (`19`) | 11 px | 500 | 1 | 0 |

Pattern: every card uses a **title + one-line subtitle** pair (19/500 over 13/400 grey), then the metric, then the visual. Deltas (`5.1% from last month`) mix two colors in one 12 px line: the number colored, the trailing words grey.

## 3. Spacing

Base unit **4 px**; the layout runs on an 8 px rhythm.

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `space-1` | 4 px | icon-to-label in dense rows, legend dot gap |
| `space-2` | 8 px | label-to-value, list row gap, chip padding-y |
| `space-3` | 12 px | title-to-subtitle block, inner row padding |
| `space-4` | 16 px | gap between cards in a row/column, nav item padding-x |
| `space-5` | 20 px | card inner padding (small cards) |
| `space-6` | 24 px | card inner padding (standard), app-area padding |
| `space-8` | 32 px | sidebar padding, header-to-first-card |

Measured specifics
- App shell inset from canvas: **≈40 px** all round; shell corner radius 24 px.
- Sidebar width **≈300 px**; content gutter left of cards **24 px**.
- Card grid gutter: **16 px** horizontal and vertical, consistently.
- Card padding: **24 px** on the large ones (balance chart, card widget, transaction history), **20 px** on the short tip/limit cards.
- Header row height **≈56 px**; search field height 48 px; icon buttons 48 × 48 px.
- Nav row height **≈44 px**, 8 px between rows, icon-label gap 12 px. Sub-nav (`History`, `Integration`, `Reports`) indented 36 px with a 1 px L-shaped tree rule.
- Section divider above `Learning center`: 1 px rule with 24 px space above and below.
- Title → subtitle: 4 px. Subtitle → metric: 12–16 px. Metric → chart: 20–24 px.
- List rows (categories, transactions): 26–28 px tall, ~10 px between.
- Quick-action tiles: 48 × 48 px, 12 px gap, label 8 px below.
- Quick-payment avatars: 44 × 44 px, 8 px gap.
- Progress bars: 10 px tall; segmented cost bar 10 px tall with 4 px gaps between segments.
- Spread bar (`ui/SpreadBar`, card scale): 4 px tall, three segments — done `--fill-progress`, in progress `--fill-live`, to do `--ink3` — with 2 px gaps and
  an optional 12 px `--ink3` caption under it; a zero segment is not drawn, and all three at zero draw nothing.

## 4. Radii

| Token | Value | Applied to |
|---|---|---|
| `radius-xs` | 4 px | bar-chart column caps, cost-analysis segments, legend swatches |
| `radius-sm` | 8 px | progress bars (pill-ish at 10 px height), small logo tiles |
| `radius-md` | 12 px | chips (`7d`, `January`), quick-action tiles, avatars, tooltip, badges on avatars |
| `radius-lg` | 16 px | cards, sidebar promo card, debit/credit card faces, search field, header icon buttons, Upgrade button, nav active row |
| `radius-xl` | 24 px | outer app shell |
| `radius-full` | 999 px | notification badge `19`, legend dots, avatar in header |

Nothing is fully square; the smallest radius anywhere is 4 px. Rounding scales with element size — a consistent ratio of roughly 1:8 (radius : shortest side) at the card level.

## 5. Elevation & strokes

- Cards sit on `--bg-app` with a **1 px `--border` stroke and no shadow** (or an imperceptible `0 1px 2px rgba(0,0,0,.03)`).
- Only two things float: the chart **tooltip** (`0 8px 24px rgba(0,0,0,.10)`, white, 12 px radius) and the **app shell** against the grey canvas (`0 24px 64px rgba(0,0,0,.10)`).
- Chart gridlines: 1 px `#EFEFEE`, horizontal only, no vertical lines, no axis line.
- Active nav is marked by a 3 px black bar flush to the left edge of the row plus the `--bg-subtle` fill — not by a color change in the text.
- Drop targets for widgets use a 1 px dashed `--border-strong` outline with 16 px radius.

---

## 6. Iconography

Outline icons, ~1.5 px stroke, 20 px box in nav and 20–22 px in tiles, rounded joins, `--text-primary` color. Chevrons and the `>` in `Read more` are 12–14 px. Icons never take accent color.

---

## 7. Reusable patterns

- **Metric card**: title (19/500) → subtitle (13/400 grey) → figure (30/700) → delta line (12 px, colored number + grey words) → visual.
- **Filter chip**: 13/500 label + chevron, 12 px radius, 1 px `--border-strong`, white fill, sits top-right of the card it filters.
- **Progress row**: name + `current / target` (target in grey), 10 px hatched bar over `--bg-track`, 11 px grey caption under.
- **Two-tone amount**: spent value in `--text-primary`, ceiling in `--text-tertiary`, same size — used for limits and goals alike.
- **Ramp encoding**: category order follows the orange → yellow → green ramp, so the segmented bar and the legend read as one object.

## 8. Applying this to the board

§8 is written new, not copied, for the same reason the dashboard's own §8 is: that section applies §1–7 to sessions, usage, analytics and management — pages this board does not have — and nothing in it draws a kanban column, an item card, a run, a stage track or a watchdog console. What follows is this app's own reading of §§2–6, in the dashboard's register: a number, the rule it sets, then the reason it was set. Two lessons the dashboard's own §8 carries apply to every subsection below, so they are stated once here rather than per subsection: nothing on `--board` uses `--strip-hi` as its only separator — on daylight the two sit two points apart and the element vanishes — and a raised control marks a state, never a button.

### 8.0 Shell and rail

The rail is the dashboard's own shipped rail, figure for figure, because the two apps sit in neighbouring tabs and a reader switching between them should see one sidebar rather than two that nearly agree. Until 2026-10-01 this board drew its own 280 px rail with 32/16 padding and an `--ink3` sub-nav, and the first side-by-side read the difference as a bug. Where the dashboard's shipped CSS departs from the spec/mock the two apps share, this board departs with it — recorded here so the next reader does not "correct" either app back to the mock's numbers without the other:

| | Spec / mock | This board | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rail width | 300 px | **240 px**, 24 px padding all round, rows 4 px apart | the dashboard's shipped rail (`.rail{flex:0 0 240px;gap:4px;padding:24px}` in its `@media (min-width:640px)` block). This board was 280 px — its own figure between the two — and that 40 px was the most visible of the differences when switching tabs |
| Nav row height | 44 px | **36 px** (44 px on the phone menu) | the dashboard's own rail CSS ships 36 px rows at 14/500 — 8 px shorter than the mock, and lighter than its own DESIGN.md prose still records — and this spec copies the shipped rule rather than the drifted prose; the mock's full 44 px is kept for the phone menu, where the row is a thumb target rather than a pointer one |
| Sub-nav indent | 36 px (the icon's right edge) | **24 px** stem (the icon's centreline: 16 px row padding + half of a 16 px icon), label 20 px past it | the dashboard's: `.rail-sub{margin:2px 0 2px 24px}` hangs the stem, `.rail-sublink{padding:7px 0 7px 20px}` sets the label, and its tick is 12 px in `--hairline2`. Rows are 14/400 `--ink2` at rest and 14/500 `--ink` for the open view — this board's old `--ink3` rows beside an `--ink` one read as disabled rather than as not chosen |

The wordmark is the dashboard's too — its prompt cursor (design handoff "1h"): a `BACKLOG` kicker over `› manager▌`, in the one face (§1 — no mono). The 11/400 uppercase `.rail-kicker` (`.16em` tracking, `--ink3`) sits 4 px over `.rail-prompt` at 22/600 (`-.03em`), whose `›` caret and 10 × 22 cursor (1 px radius) are both `--green-solid`, 8 px from the word, with 24 px of padding under the whole. The cursor is the one part that moves: it blinks (`steps(1)` over 1.06 s) while a run on this machine is working — `runsWorking` (`client/src/lib/run-pulse.ts`): a `starting` entry, or a local run that is `running` and not crashed, never a paused or a remote one — and stands solid otherwise, Settings included. It is a hint and never the record; `RunChip` and the Runs page say it in words. `App` polls nothing for it: Board, Runs and Archive each already run a `useOrchestratorRuns()` and report the answer up (`useReportWorking`), and `App` drops the report on a section change so a view that unmounted cannot keep it blinking. The `<h1>` is named `Backlog Manager` by `aria-label` and its drawn parts are `aria-hidden`. In the phone bar the prompt drops to 17 px with an 8 × 17 cursor 6 px from the word and 3 px under the kicker, which stays at 11 px where the dashboard's bar draws 10 — guard 3's floor. Every `<button>` in the rail draws Hanken Grotesk because of one reset rule — `button, input, select, textarea { font-family: inherit; font-variant-numeric: inherit }` — which design guard 2 pins: without it the UA's own `font` shorthand drew every rail row and every card chip in Arial, and that, more than any figure in this table, is why the two rails looked different.

Rows are 14/500, 16 px padding-x, 16 px radius, a 16 px outline icon 12 px from the label. Active is a 3 px ink bar 16 px tall at the left edge plus a `--strip-hi` fill — never a colour change in the label (§5). Hover darkens the label and nothing else; the fill stays reserved for active, so a hovered row and a selected row read as two different things rather than one filled twice. Icons hold `--ink` at every row weight (§6) — they name the section, never its state.

The sub-nav tree is drawn only for the open section. Runs gets the first one this board has, History and Watchdog (§8.4) — the same choice `RUNS_MODE_KEY` (`client/src/lib/runs-mode.ts`) already persists and guards, whose segmented control is replaced by the tree at every width, not only above the phone breakpoint. Settings' Local and Shared (§8.6) and Jira's Board and Setup (§8.9) follow it, one `RailTree` drawing all three, and each tree is its section's only page switch.

Since Jira phase 2 the rail lists five sections — Board, Runs, Archive, Jira, Settings, in `SECTIONS` order — but draws the Jira row only while `GET /api/jira` says the site is configured (or while a stored `jira` section is being held for that answer, §8.9). A machine without Jira credentials sees the four rows it always had, and nothing in the rail moves to make room for an absent one.

Settings sits under a 1 px rule with 24 px of air either side: it is about the board, the rows above it — four, or five with Jira — are about the work. `.main` sits on `--board` with a 24 px top-left radius — the one corner the rail and the shell strip meet — and no top margin: the strip above it is the gap.

Below 700 px the rail becomes a top bar, wordmark left and ☰ right, with every tree standing open so a sub-view is a tap away rather than behind an accordion. The bar hides on a downward scroll and returns on the first upward one, and stays pinned while the menu is open, because the menu hangs off it.

**Shell strip.** Right of the rail, `.maincol` stacks a 50 px `.topstrip` over the well, both on `--strip` — the rail's own paper, not a new token — so the rail and the strip read as one white L around the `--board` well, and the well's 24 px top-left curve is finally drawn against white (a top margin on `.main` drew it grey on grey, because `body` is `--board` too, and `body` stays so). The strip's height, the well's gap and what `.runs-board` subtracts to fill the viewport are one token, `--main-gap` (50 px, `0px` on the phone), and design guard 9 pins it. The strip holds what is true of the machine wherever you stand — the dashboard's rule for its account header — which today is up to two things, right-aligned: the tracker chip, and since Jira phase 2 the Jira chip after it (§8.9.4), each drawn only while its service is set up; with neither the strip is drawn empty at full height, so the well's top edge never moves.

The tracker chip (`TrackerChip`) is the dashboard's `.acct` pill: a 36 px bare pill, transparent at rest and `--strip-hi` on hover or while open; a 26 px `--strip-hi` avatar with the login's initial (`—` without a token) and a 7 px pip only in two states — `--amber-solid` for no token, `--red` when some repo is failing, nothing when all is healthy; the login at 12.5/600; a `POLL` meter counting down to the server's next sweep (full `--amber-solid` bar reading `overdue` after two silent cycles, full `--red` reading `failing` when every repo fails, empty reading `…` before the first poll); an `API` meter for the hourly budget (`<1%` floor, amber from 60 %, red from 90 %); an 11 px `Chevron` (`client/src/components/ui/Chevron.tsx`) that points `up` while open and `down` otherwise. Both meters are `Meter` (§7's meter row at chip size): label over value, **both 11 px** where the dashboard draws a 9.5 px label and a 9 px caret — guard 3's floor — so the meter is 56 px rather than 52, and it grows to its content rather than wrapping when a value like `overdue` is wider. Its popover is `Popover` (§8.7) at 420 px (`--strip`, a hairline border, 12 px radius, 16 px padding, 8 px below the chip, and the small `0 8px 24px` lift rather than the dialogs' `0 24px 64px`): identity, one row per connected repo with its own 120 px line timer (a failing repo draws a full red bar carrying the access sentence), and the API bar with `N of M left · resets HH:MM`. No links and no buttons in it: the rail is the only thing that switches sections and Settings' pages. Below 700 px the strip is not drawn and the chip sits in the rail bar between the wordmark and ☰ — one element in one home, chosen by `useNarrow` in JS. There it drops the login whenever meters follow it (#229): the 351 px bar has no room for a reading whose width tracks the account, the avatar still carries the initial and the popover the full login, and ☰ is `flex: none` so it never yields its 36 px. The no-token chip keeps its `no token` label — it draws no meters and fits. **Two chips in the bar drop their meters.** When the tracker chip and the Jira chip share the bar, neither draws `.tracker-meters` — avatar, pip and caret only — because with both, even without their logins, the row measured ~178 px over the bar's 351 px content box, and every item but ☰ being `flex: none`, ☰ was pushed past `.rail`'s `overflow-x: hidden` and clipped: the failure #229 fixed for one chip, and the same rule decides it — ☰ is the phone's only section switch and never gives up space. The pip still carries the one reading that must not hide (red, access failing), and each popover prints every meter one tap away. A lone chip keeps its meters, and the desktop strip is outside the rule entirely.

Every disclosure mark is the one `Chevron` (`client/src/components/ui/Chevron.tsx`) — a stroked SVG in `currentColor`, 11 px here (the dashboard's is 9), `dir` `down`, `up` or `right` as rotations of one path, the 160 ms turn off under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Today that is the tracker chip's caret and the dispatch control's mark; `test/chevrons.test.ts` fails any component or `content:` rule that draws a text triangle (`▸ ▾ ▴ ◂ ▶ ▼`) instead.

### 8.1 Type

| Role | Size / weight | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Page and card title | 19/500, −.01em, 1.3 | band titles, sheet titles, the Runs detail sheet's project |
| Large metric | 30/700, −.02em, 1.1 | figure strip values |
| Row name | 15/500 | column header names |
| Card title | 14/500, 1.35 | item card title, Runs list row project (Live and History), stage track item title |
| Subtitle / hint | 13/400 | band subtitle, sheet subtitle, figure label, control label |
| Body, chip and button label | 13 (500 on buttons) | filter chips, controls |
| Meta / axis / caption | 12/400 | `id · date`, project name beside its dot, stage durations, table time column |
| Pill, badge, status | 11/500 | count pills, markers, mode pill, stage word on the live strip |

11 px is the floor beneath all of these; nothing on the board goes smaller.

Hanken Grotesk is the one face — self-hosted via `@fontsource/hanken-grotesk`, weights 400, 500, 600 and 700 imported in `client/src/main.tsx` — rather than linked the way the dashboard's own foundation links Google Fonts. That is the one place this section departs from the dashboard's: the served build's CSP is `default-src 'self'` with no `font-src`, `style-src` is pinned exactly by `test/csp.test.ts`, and the tailnet/phone use case this board is built for wants no third-party fetch at all.

`--mono` and `--display` are deleted from `theme.css`, not aliased to `--font` — an alias would let a stale `var(--mono)` left behind by the redesign keep quietly resolving to Plex. Deleting it means the same rule instead falls back to the inherited face, which is the failure this board wants to surface rather than hide. `code, kbd, samp, pre { font-family: inherit }` removes the UA's own monospace default for the same reason, so a code span is marked by its fill and ink alone, and `body` carries `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` so figures line up wherever they appear. `text-transform: uppercase` survives on `.rail-kicker` alone.

Three faces — Barlow, Barlow Condensed and IBM Plex Mono — left behind 80 `--mono` uses, 14 `--display` uses and 31 `text-transform: uppercase` rules in `client/src/styles.css`, and 124 of that file's 130 `font-size` declarations sat at 12.5 px or smaller. `task-36` cleared all of them: the two tokens and the uppercasing are gone outright (`test/design-guards.test.ts`'s guards 1 and 4 hold them there), and every size below the 11 px floor was raised to the role this table gives it. The sizes at or above 11 px that the table does not name by element were left where they were, because each sits on a surface tasks 2–5 redraw and the primitives those tasks compose carry the full table already.

### 8.2 Tokens and themes

`shared/theme.css` keeps its vocabulary; daylight's values are rewritten to the reference palette, the same values the dashboard's own daylight block already carries:

| Token(s) | Daylight |
|---|---|
| `--board` / `--steel` / `--strip` / `--strip-hi` | `#f4f4f3` / `#ededec` / `#ffffff` / `#f2f2f1` |
| `--edge` / `--hairline` / `--hairline2` | `rgba(0,0,0,.03)` / `#eaeae8` / `#dcdcda` |
| `--ink` / `--ink2` / `--ink3` | `#131313` / `#525252` / `#6b6b6b` |
| `--green` / `--amber` / `--mustard` / `--cyan` / `--red` / `--magenta` | `#38631a` / `#c4761f` / `#5f530b` / `#306c0f` / `#b03b28` / `#6b52a8` |
| `--green-solid` / `--amber-solid` / `--amber-text` / `--amber-ink` / `--live-ink` | `#7dc242` / `#c4761f` / `var(--ink)` / `#8a5214` / `var(--amber-ink)` |
| `--on-accent` / `--scrim` / `--shadow` / `--shadow2` | `#ffffff` / `rgba(19,19,19,.28)` / `rgba(0,0,0,.06)` / `rgba(0,0,0,.1)` |

Every token set as text holds WCAG AA (4.5:1) on all four surfaces, and that is why several values are not the reference palette's. The palette's own `#6e6e6e` / `#a0a0a0` greys and the first daylight status hues — `--green` `#5fa92c` (`--green-600`), `--amber` `#c4761f`, `--mustard` `#9a8712`, `--cyan` `#3f8f14` — measured 2.6–4.35:1 on paper; the values above are the dashboard's AA set, and `test/daylight-contrast.test.ts` recomputes every pair from `shared/theme.css`, the tint sites read off `styles.css` at their own percentage and surface. A second case reads off `styles.css` every token a rule actually sets a word in and holds it to the same line, so a word set in a fill or a mark goes red the day it lands; a rule whose `background` paints something is measured against that ground instead — reverse type on a one-token fill by a third case, words on a tint by the tint case — while `background: none` or `transparent` spares nothing; it spares only a ground it cannot compute (a gradient), the `●` dots and the wordmark's caret (marks drawn as glyphs, closed by selector), and the `--proj-N` identity hues, which are not status colours.

Marks and text part ways here, under the dashboard's names, so the two repos keep one vocabulary. A status hue is both the ink a word is set in and the fill of a dot, meter, bar or border; on the dark themes one bright value does both, but on paper no green or amber light enough to look like itself clears 4.5:1 as text. So `--green` is the text and `--green-solid` the mark (`--green-500`, the same value as `--fill-progress` — two tokens because they answer different questions; it is 2.2:1 on `--strip`, and every green mark the board draws has its word beside it). Amber's mark is `--amber-solid`, the bright `#c4761f`, 3:1 on every surface a mark sits on. Amber text has no value that is both AA and amber-looking on white, so it takes one of two tokens by what is around it: `--amber-text`, the plain ink, where a neighbouring amber mark or pill already says "warning" (the crashed Live row's line under its `⚠ crashed` pill, the Watchdog card's line holding its attempt pips); `--amber-ink`, a brown `#8a5214`, where the word must carry the hue itself — on an amber tint (the warn pill, the stage chip, the notes and flags) and wherever nothing amber stands beside it (markers, the status word, sheet errors, the degraded-scan line). `--mustard` and `--cyan` stay single tokens, dark enough as text to serve as marks too. The live and paused status words — `running`, `paused`, the Watchdog feed's live kind — read `--live-ink`, `--fill-live`'s text twin: they were set in the fill itself, 2.07:1 on `--strip`, until the branch that made daylight the default. Daylight points it at `--amber-ink` rather than a new brown, so `running` and `aborted` share a colour here exactly as they already do on every dark theme, and the word tells them apart.

On the four dark themes each new token is derived, never declared: a `:root` block in `shared/theme.css`, placed above the daylight block so daylight overrides it, sets `--green-solid` to `var(--green)`, the three amber tokens to `var(--amber)` and `--live-ink` to `var(--fill-live)` — so every dark theme computes exactly what it did before the split. The board's tints keep their own mix per site (6–14 % over `--strip` or `--steel`) rather than a `--X-tint` token per hue as the dashboard has, because one token per hue would move every dark theme's pill fill; each site only mixes the mark token of its hue. `client/src/styles.css` reads bare `var(--amber)` nowhere.

Two tokens are new, added to every theme block, because the design draws two things in the ramp's bright fills and the palette otherwise has no fill that is not also a text colour:

| Token | Meaning | Daylight | Dark themes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--fill-live` | the card's live strip, the current stage node, the "you are here" bar | `#F5A15C` (`--orange-400`, §2) | `var(--amber)` |
| `--fill-progress` | filled progress, a reached stage node | `#7DC242` (`--green-500`, §2) | `var(--green)` |
| `--on-fill` | ink ON either of those two fills | `var(--ink)` | `var(--on-accent)` |

`--on-fill` is `task-37`'s, added when the card's live strip first set type on one of those fills: `--ink` is right on daylight (`#131313` on `#F5A15C`, 7.3:1) and wrong on the four dark themes, where it is near-white against a bright amber — 1.4:1 on midnight at 11 px. `--on-accent` fails the other way, because on daylight the accents are dark and so that token is white. So the third token is the dark end of whichever palette declares it, and §8.3's "11/500 `--ink` text" is read through it.

Each dark theme block declares its own `var(--amber)` / `var(--green)` line for these rather than sharing one rule across all four, the same way every other ramp token is themed per block. Beyond these two lines, the four dark theme blocks are otherwise unchanged as overrides — no other dark-theme rule in this section is touched.

`.hatch` is one utility rule over any fill: `repeating-linear-gradient(45deg, rgba(255,255,255,.30) 0 1px, transparent 1px 4px)`.

The four column dots read the design's category ramp left to right — refactors `--amber`, ideas `--mustard`, bugs `--red`, tasks `--green` — replacing today's magenta/mustard/red/cyan ticks. The refactors tick is `--magenta`'s last *column* job, but not its last job: the capture dispatch control still reads it. That was four of the token's five uses in the stylesheet — `.dispatch-tab.capture` and `.dispatch-chip.capture`, base and hover — against the tick's one; `task-37` collapsed the control's two shapes into one `Chip` whose action tone paints the WORD, so the four became `.dispatch-word.capture` alone and the tick survived on Archive's own column header until `task-39` put that surface on `BoardColumn` too, which left `.dispatch-word.capture` as the token's one remaining reader. The dispatch chip keeps its three action tones exactly as coded, unchanged by this redesign: groom `--mustard`, execute `--cyan`, capture `--magenta`. Ideas now sharing mustard with the groom chip costs nothing — a column dot and a card's own control are never read as the same object.

The project-hue rule carries over unchanged: one 8 px dot, on the card and on the modal's facts column, never on text — the project name sits in `--ink2` beside it. The eight `--proj-N` sets are themselves untouched; the dot is applied by class, never a `style` attribute, so a theme swap recolours it for free.

The one-home rule for primitives (the design spec's §12.1) applies to every pattern this section draws that more than one surface uses: each is one component in `client/src/components/ui/`, owning one class family in `styles.css`, so two surfaces can never restate — or quietly disagree about — the same look. Until `docs/subsystems/board.md` carries this rule, as the spec's own §11 intends, the spec's §12 table is the pointer for which primitive owns which pattern.

#### 8.4.1 History

**Why the name.** `History` names the rail destination — Runs › History — not the contents of the page under it. That page carries four things and only one of them is past: the figures, the live runs, the past runs, and the selected run whole. It is named for what a person is looking for when they arrive with nothing running, and because a tree whose first leaf repeats its own parent teaches nothing.

**The band.** `Runs` at 19/500 over a 13 px `--ink2` line that counts all three states at once — `3 live · 1 starting · 28 past` — then, right, the project select and the range control as chips. The range control is `RUN_RANGES`' four steps (`client/src/lib/run-range.ts:41`) as one segmented chip, and the sheet subtitles below name whichever is in force.

**The figure strip, directly under the band — the statistics lead.** One sheet the ground divides: five figures on a 2 px `--board` gap grid, wrapping five → three → two under 1100 and under 700 with the seam drawn in both directions, so the gap reads as one grid at every width rather than as five cards that happen to touch. Each cell is a label at 13 `--ink2`, a value at 30/700, and a 12 px line under it. All five carry that line where today only `runs` and `avg item work` do (`client/src/components/runs/RunsView.tsx:999`, `:1041`), and the three new ones say what the figure above them *means* — which is the only thing a line here is allowed to be. The five read what `aggregateRuns` returns (`client/src/lib/run-stats.ts:653`) and nothing else, and that is what rules out the three lines the mock had drawn instead: `+4 vs prior 30 days` compares against a prior period nothing computes, `3 parked` counts something the aggregate does not keep, and `1 red of 41` was invented and in the wrong unit besides.

- **runs** — its line the five-status breakdown, glyph and count each, in `STATUS_ORDER`'s order (`client/src/lib/runs-list.ts`).
- **completed / queued** — line `merged or branched`, which is the definition of completed rather than a gloss on it.
- **avg item work** — line `queue wait excluded`, the same rule `itemDurationMs` already enforces (`client/src/lib/run-time.ts:369`).
- **rework / completed** — the one-decimal ratio `RunAggregates.fixLoopsPerMerged` (`run-stats.ts:588`) over the line `fix loops per completed item`, with today's long sentence kept as the cell's `title`. The mock's `6 / 41` fraction needs two numbers the aggregate does not return.
- **verify pass** — a rate over verification *runs*, not items, and its line `of every verification run` says which, because the unit is the whole claim.

The wide machine-time tile becomes the strip's **sixth cell, full width**, holding `StageBars` (`client/src/components/runs/StageBars.tsx:42`) redrawn at a 10 px bar height — up from the 6 px `.run-bars-track` ships today (`client/src/styles.css:2272`), because at full width the bar is now the cell's subject rather than a sparkline beside a number. Always seven rows, in pipeline order, `—` for a stage never recorded: the seven are `MACHINE_STAGES` (`run-stats.ts:276`), and a stage that drops out when it has no total turns the chart into a different chart per range. Its line is `<range> · queue wait excluded`. The whole strip hides with the list when the range or project filter empties it, as today.

**The split**, under the strip: a **420 px** list column on the left carrying the Live sheet and then the History sheet, and one detail sheet on the right showing the selected run. Under **1100** px the two columns stack, list first, the detail a scroll away; under **700** px the sheets are full width. 420 px is the figure the row designs below answer to — every reading that does not survive that width is in the detail sheet instead, and the list is what has to stay legible when the detail is a scroll away.

**Wide** (2026-09-17): once the BOARD is **1400 px** or wider, the page becomes three columns — the band spanning the top, the split holding the first column exactly as above, and the figure strip leaving the top of the page to stand as a **320 px rail** on the right, one figure wide, its five cells over the range's machine-time bars, top-aligned and scrolling itself when the six exceed the board. Nothing else moves: the list stays 420 px, the rows and the detail body are unchanged, and under 1400 px the page is byte-identical to the paragraph above. The bars' label and value columns narrow to 72/56 in the rail so the track keeps about 140 px, below which a seven-row chart stops being comparable — which is the only thing the chart is for.

The threshold is 420 + ~600 + 320 + two `--group-gap`, where ~600 px is the width below which the detail's seven-node stage track stops being legible. The 1280 px `.wrap.wide` cap sits under it deliberately, so the rail is reachable only with content width set to full, and there only on a window around 1728 px or wider at 100 % text.

It is a **container query on a measuring wrapper the History page renders around itself** (`.runs-frame`), and both halves of that are load-bearing. A media query is the wrong instrument: the board's width is the window minus the 240 px rail, minus `--body-pad` twice, minus whatever the measure cap is doing, all divided by `.shell`'s `zoom` — at 120 % text a 1920 px window leaves at most 1312 CSS px of board (1600 − 240 − 48), which is not room for three columns and which `@media (min-width: 1920px)` would fill with them anyway. And the container is that wrapper rather than `.wrap` or `.main` because `container-type: inline-size` applies layout containment, which makes the element the containing block for every `position: fixed` descendant: the item modal and the form sheets (§8.7) are fixed and render inside `.wrap` with no portal, so a container up there would pin them to the section instead of the viewport. Nothing under Runs is fixed today; whatever becomes so must portal to `body`. Watchdog renders no wrapper — it has no strip to stand as a rail. The strip and the band are placed through the page's own classes (`.runs-stats`, `.runs-band`) passed to the primitives as `className`, the contract `Sheet` already had (the design spec's §12.1), never by selecting `.ui-figure-strip` from the page.

**The detail sheet.** One run, whole, beside the list.

Its **head** is the project (a `Dot` plus 19/500), the run id at 12 `--ink3`, the item count when the run is finished, and `RunControls` (`client/src/components/RunControls.tsx:70`) right as 28 px chips — unchanged in what it offers: running and fresh gets **Pause**; a run with a pause requested gets the note `Pausing after <id>` (`RunControls.tsx:116`) beside **Cancel**, which withdraws the request and takes no accent, because nothing here stops a run and a control that is not destructive must not read as one; `paused` gets **Resume run**, hidden when the environment cannot spawn, `aria-disabled` with the reason as its title when the project is not visible, `Resuming…` in flight — behind that word `RunControls` now carries the synchronous in-flight guard `RunStrip`'s own Resume used to (bug-19's first layer, the table below): the board's `resuming` mark is set only once the request has already answered, so for the whole gap before that nothing else stands watch, and without this guard a click repeated into that gap fires a second request the first has not yet answered; a crashed run gets **Resume run** exactly when `watchdogStoodDown` (`shared/agent.ts:695`) says the sweeper will not — and `test/watchdog-coupling.test.tsx`, which drives that coupling from one table of hand-checked verdicts, drives this head from now on rather than the strip that is leaving. **Stop** sits beside Pause, beside Cancel and on a crashed run, and it asks first (bug-53): the chip is replaced in place by `Confirm` (`client/src/components/ui/Confirm.tsx`) — the question at 12/400 in full `--ink`, naming the item in flight and that the run cannot be resumed, then a `danger` **Stop run** and a `flat` **Keep running**, the second holding the focus — so a misclick one chip over from Pause costs a second click rather than a run. 1. #### 8.4.2 Watchdog

**The band.** `Runs · Watchdog`, the `section · destination` convention the tree sets, with `stateLine`'s own sentence (`run-watchdog.ts:154`) as its 13 px subtitle — `armed — watching <ids>, next check in Ns`, `idle — no running run`, `off — <reason>`, each gaining `· resume disabled` when the switch is off. Right, one flat `Policy in Settings ›` chip: the policy is read here and edited there, and a second editor for the same three numbers is a second thing to keep in agreement.

**Three figures.** **Sweeper** — the phase as a 24/700 word rather than a number, since there is no quantity here, with a 6 px sweep meter under it in `--fill-progress` carrying `.hatch` (§8.2) and the line `next sweep in 4m 12s · every 10m`; the meter draws only while armed. **Watching** — the count of running runs over the app's own three counts, `⚠ 1 crashed · 2 fresh · 1 not yet watched`, the last of them drawn only when the runs payload is a tick ahead of the sweeper (`WatchdogMonitor.tsx:193-208`). Those three words are the tile's own and not a paraphrase of them: what a reader needs here is how many runs the sweeper holds an opinion about and how many it does not yet, and a count this tile cannot name is the one thing it must never print. **Policy** — `check every` / `leave alone for` / `give up after` as three label-over-value pairs, 12 over 14/500, with the enabled switch's state as the 12 px line. Both the sweeper line's cadence and all three policy values are the saved config's, not constants; their bounds are `WATCHDOG_LIMITS` (`shared/types.ts:1029`), and the Settings ladders and the server's clamp already read that one triple.

Two switches reach this page and they are not interchangeable, which is why the Policy line names its own — in the mock's words, `resume spawns on · your switch, not the phase`. `WatchdogConfig.enabled` is the user's Settings toggle and withholds the **spawn** alone: a watchdog disabled there still arms, still ticks, and still reports the crashed run it would have resumed. The phase reads `off` only when the environment says so — `offReason()` returns `BM_WATCHDOG off` or `BM_AGENTS off` and nothing else (`server/src/agents/watchdog.service.ts:228-232`, whose own comment records that `enabled` is deliberately not folded in) — and it is that state, an operator's rather than a user's, which hides the Watching sheet below and puts `nothing is watched while off` in this tile.

**The Watching sheet.** One row per run in the runs payload whose status is `running`, annotated from `watching` exactly as today — skew rendered, not hidden. Each row carries a `Dot`, the project at 14/500, the run id at 12 `--ink3`, `· not yet watched` when the payload is a tick ahead of the sweeper (`client/src/components/runs/WatchdogMonitor.tsx:271`), the verdict as glyph plus word (`● ok` / `⚠ crashed`), `last reported <id> · <stage>` or `between items`, and the heartbeat meter — the age against `RUN_STALE_MS` (`shared/types.ts:767`), amber once past it — under its two labels. A crashed row adds the attempts dots, the `watchdogClause` sentence, `→ session <id>`, and `leave alone Nm more` while the grace clock runs. A `Resume now` chip is drawn **only** when `watchdogStoodDown` allows — never while the sweeper will still spawn (attempts left AND, since bug-35, refusals under the same ceiling), whatever the grace clock says, because that is the one function both this chip and §8.4.1's detail head read. A watched id with no run in the payload renders as a placeholder line rather than as nothing: a set the console cannot explain is the failure this page exists to show.

The meter's first label is `heartbeat Ns ago` (`{beat}`, `WatchdogMonitor.tsx:311`) — a numeral every time. Its second label carries none: `stale at <RUN_STALE_MS>` and `past the <RUN_STALE_MS> stale line` are both formatted from the constant at render time (`WatchdogMonitor.tsx:316-318`), and the comment standing beside them gives the reason (`:312-314`): a meter labelled with a typed literal keeps naming the old window the day `RUN_STALE_MS` moves, and on that day the label lies while the meter behind it is still right. The same rule is why this section names the constant instead of its value.

The row is a button, and it **jumps to History and selects the run in its detail sheet** — which is what `WatchdogMonitor` does today (`onSelectRun`, `WatchdogMonitor.tsx:79`), and under option D is the only place it could send anyone: the run's detail *is* that sheet.

**The Activity sheet.** The event ledger keeps today's five columns and full run ids — `time · kind · project · run · what the sweeper did` — with `th` at 12/400 `--ink2`, `td` at 13, and the time column at 12 `--ink3`. Its subtitle keeps the caveat rather than burying it: the last `WATCHDOG_EVENT_CAP` events only (`shared/types.ts:1111`), held in the API process's memory, emptied by a restart. The kind cell is `WATCHDOG_KIND_GLYPH` plus the word, coloured by `WATCHDOG_KIND_TONE` (both `run-watchdog.ts:251` and `:271`) — `live` and `done` in `--green`, `bad` in `--red`, `warn` in `--amber`, `muted` in `--ink3`; seven kinds share those five tones, and the word beside the glyph is always the readable answer. The sheet scrolls inside itself under 700 px rather than letting five columns set the page's width.

**Why it stays a page.** Folding the console into the run rows was drawn twice and picked against both times, and the reason is what the console is for: it is read *during* a run, while the reader is already looking at that run, and its Watching set is the **sweeper's** set — one poll tick of skew away from the runs payload by design. Rendered as annotations on the run rows, that skew would read as the list being wrong; rendered as its own set, with `· not yet watched` on the rows that differ, it reads as the one true fact it is.

### 8.8 Motion

Four places on this board move on their own, and one mechanism covers all four rather than four separate ones:

- The **wordmark's cursor** (§8.0) blinks while a run on this machine is working — `.rail-cursor.blink`, the one class `SideRail` adds from `working`.

Two apply everywhere: the mocks' issue counts are illustrative and were not recomputed, and a path under `$HOME` reads `~` (`displayPath`, from the status's own `home`). Every derivation a Jira surface prints — the band line, the card's crumb and age, a board row's sentence, the chip's meters — has one home in `client/src/lib/jira.ts`, and every poll or check age goes through `pollAge` (`client/src/lib/tracker.ts`), so a Jira poll reads `12 s` exactly as a GitHub one does, and a board never swept reads `not polled yet` wherever an age would be. The browser never talks to Jira: no avatar image, no fetched link, and the one link out on each surface is the issue's `url` exactly as the server built it, in a new tab with no referrer.

While the shell is holding a stored `jira` section for the status's answer (§8.0), the section draws the Board's own two lines in the Board's `board-empty` shape — `loading…` before any answer, `Jira unavailable` after a failed one — rather than the shell's Suspense spinner, because the chunk has arrived and it is the status that has not.

#### 8.9.1 Board

Cards in columns, `BoardB.html`: §8.3's Board read as a Jira board, with the parent as a crumb on the card. Rejected: the roots as groups with their subtasks nested under them (`Main.dc.html`) — the hierarchy took over the column's job, and a column has to say one thing.

**The fold** (`GroupTop.dc.html`, G-A; grouped-board spec §3). Every issue whose parent is on the loaded list folds into its topmost on-board ancestor, at any depth, and only those tops are cards — `foldBoard` in `lib/jira.ts` is the one walk of the parent chain, and a cycle draws its members side by side. A top sits in its own category's column, whatever its folded issues' columns are, and tops sort by the newest `updated` across the top and everything under it (`sortTops`), so work on a child lifts its parent; the card's age stays the top's own. Rejected: G-B, folding only subtasks — stories stayed cards under their epic, 17 issues drawing as 9 cards in the mock against G-A's 5; G-C, an inline expand toggle on the card — the modal is the one way into the children (§8.9.2); G-D, a child whose column differs from its top's surfacing there as a dashed card — the card's spread bar says that instead.

**The band.** `Band` titled with the board's name, and under it `bandSub`'s line — `acme.atlassian.net · 14 issues from 2 roots and 1 query · polled 12 s ago` — with a zero source omitted (`1 query`, never `0 roots`), then `M cards` when the fold made fewer cards than issues — both counts from the loaded list, unfiltered, so a line that says `17 issues · 5 cards` counts one list (before the list has loaded the count is the status's `board.count` and there is no cards part), `first 500 shown` last whenever the cap bit, and Jira's own message for the first failure (`+N more` past one) when the last sweep could not read a root or a query, the whole line `--red` through `.jira-band-sub.failing` so each theme's red is the red. The age is the newer of the two stamps the page holds, the board list's own and the status's, and ticks every second in a component of its own, so the clock re-renders one line and not every card. Right-aligned: the board picker, the summary search (§8.3's 36 px field, placeholder `search summaries`) and the filter track. No run chip and no Orchestrate: nothing on a Jira board is a run.

**The picker.** A `Chip` naming the board — the name ellipsised at 220 px, so a long one cannot push the search and the track off the line — with the `Chevron` turning `up` while open, opening a 320 px `Popover` (§8.7) of every board by name: 36 px rows at 13/500 `--ink2`, the board in view `--ink` with a 16 px stroked tick, `--strip-hi` on hover — the Sort panel's rows. It picks and nothing else: `Edit` and `New` are Setup's (spec §1 overrides the mock that hung them here), because a picker that also edits is a second Setup page with half its controls.

**Filters.** The Board's own `FilterBar` drawn without its sort half, Archive's precedent (§8.5): one funnel, raised while a filter is set, over a 300 px panel with three sections — `Type` and `Sprint` as single-select pick chips with an "all" chip first (and a `no sprint` chip only when some loaded issue has none), `Assignee` as `anyone` / `me` / `unassigned`, `me` absent while the identity is unknown. Every option is read off the loaded list, nothing is persisted, and nothing is a request. There is no Sort control at all (spec §1 overrides the mock's): the list is in the fold's order (`sortTops`), and a sort would only reorder what the columns already make scannable. A filter or the search reaches folded issues too (F1, `GroupFilter.dc.html`): a top is shown when it matches or when anything folded under it does, and a top shown ONLY for its folded matches carries a 2 px `--amber-solid` inset ring and a `match` marker (`1 match: PAY-162`, or `N matches` with the keys in its title) — the ring answers why the card is here, so a top that matches on its own draws neither. The options still read every loaded issue, folded or not.

**Columns.** Three, not four — To Do, In Progress, Done, one per status category — as `BoardColumn`s whose slugs `todo`, `doing` and `done` are outside `RAMP_SLUGS`, so each header draws the plain dot out of scope draws on Archive (§8.5): a category is a verdict about progress, not a backlog type, and a ramp hue would put it in the type vocabulary. `.jira-board-columns` is `.board-columns` with three tracks, declared after the Board's three rules so its 1100 px step is overridden — three categories over two tracks would put Done under To Do, which reads as a fourth column rather than as the third — and below 700 px the three stack as the Board's do. A column's count pill counts cards, not issues — what the reader can see in it. A board with no issues draws its three empty columns; a site with no boards draws `No boards yet` with an `Open Setup` chip, the rail's own navigation threaded down (§8.0), never a second page switch; and a `jira.json` the server cannot read draws `jira.json cannot be read — <reason>` with no chip at all, because `No boards yet` would invite a person to recreate boards that are still in the file and Setup cannot repair a file that does not parse.

**The card.** `JiraCard` is §8.3's card, not a second one: `.board-card`'s ground, radius, face padding and gap, the 14/500 title and the marker row are composed, never restated, so a density or theme change to the Board's card moves this one with it. What a Jira issue adds is one row and one disc:

- The head, 12 px `--ink3`: a 14 px outline type glyph (sub-task, epic or above by hierarchy level, bug, story, else the task square; the type name is its title), the key at 500 `--ink2`, and the crumb pushed right and ellipsised — `root` for one of the board's roots, else `↳ PAY-120 Refund flows` for a parent, else `Assigned to me`, else nothing. The crumb is what gives when the row is short, because the key is the card's handle.
- The foot: a 20 px initials disc on `--strip-hi` at 11/600 (two letters, `initialsOf`; empty and dashed for an unassigned issue, so the row keeps its shape) and the name at 12/400 `--ink2`, ellipsised like the Board card's project name; the RAW status name as a `neutral` `Pill`, because `In Review` and `In Progress` are one column and two very different facts; the age at 12/400 `--ink3`; and the `↗` link out, which stops its click and its Enter and Space for the reason `ItemCard`'s own link gives. The age is an age, not a date — `4m`, `2d` — and a Done card reads the age of its resolution where it has one (spec §1 overrides the mock's `resolved Oct 11`).
- The fold block, drawn only on a top with something folded under it, after the foot and before the marker row: `C children · D below them` at 12/500 `--ink2` (`C children` when nothing is deeper; C the direct children on the board, D the rest), then the `SpreadBar` (§3) over every folded issue with its `2 done · 4 in progress · 3 to do` caption.
- The marker row, drawn only when it has something to say: F1's `match` marker first, in `--amber-text`, then the priority as `Marker tone="kind"` in `--ink3`. A priority is a fact about the issue, not a state this board colours, and Jira's own five-colour scale would be a second palette on the card. The old subtask-count marker is gone: it counted Jira subtasks on board or not, and the fold block counts what this board holds.

The whole card is a `role="button"` that opens the issue modal (§8.9.2). No `Run ▾` and no live strip in phase 2: both arrive with phase 5, and a disabled one now would be a promise the phase has not kept.

#### 8.9.2 Issue modal

`JiraIssueModal`, `IssueModal.html`: §8.7's sidecar applied to a ticket — `Modal`'s second composer and not a second modal design, so the scrim, the shell lift, the 1080 px width, the 700 px fold and the Escape stack are the shell's, the facts column is the item modal's `.item-facts` through `ui/Fact`, and the body is `.item-body` at §8.1's scale. That is the whole reason a second composer is right where §8.7 used to say there was one: it is the same reading — a facts column and a body, opened, read and closed — of a ticket instead of an item, and it holds no run state.

**Facts.** The key at 13/500 `--ink2` with `open in Jira ↗` beside it in `--cyan`, then: `type · parent`, `status` (the raw name · the category in the column's own word), `priority · sprint`, `assignee · reporter`, `labels`, `created · updated` (`Oct 2 · 4m` — a date for when it began, an age for when it last moved), `on boards`, and `repository` — the mapped paths for the issue's project, in `~` form, or the hint `no repository mapped for PAY — Jira › Setup › Repositories`, which names the place in words rather than linking to it (§8.0's rule). A fact with nothing to say draws nothing, `Fact`'s rule: `labels` with none, `priority · sprint` with neither, and `on boards` while the detail has not landed or names no board the status knows. The modal opens with every fact the card already carried, so the column is full at once; the detail read then supplies what a card cannot.

**Body.** The summary as the 19/500 body title; the description rendered through `renderMarkdown` (`client/src/lib/markdown.ts`, the sanitising renderer the item modal uses too — this text was typed by anyone with access to the site), or `no description` at 12 px `--ink3`; the Children section (below); and, always last, `Comments stay in Jira — v1 neither reads nor writes them.` with the issue's own link — spec §1's override of the first draft's comment list and composer. While the detail is in flight the body reads `loading…`; a failed read keeps the facts as they were and says `could not read the ticket — <the server's sentence>` where the body would be, because a column that blanked on a Jira blip would take the readable half down with the unreadable one.

**Children** (`ModalChildren.dc.html`; grouped-board spec §6). A head row — the `Children` h3, a `neutral` `Pill` with the direct-children count, and `N in all` pushed right at 12 px `--ink3` only when the tree draws more rows than that — then the `SpreadBar` (§3) over the rows as drawn, with its caption, then the tree. Direct children are the detail read's, fresh; under each hang its descendants from the board's fold index as it stood when the card was clicked (`modalTree`), indented 22 px a level, so the tree costs no Jira call of its own. Each key is drawn once, where the fresh read puts it, so an issue reparented since the last poll is never in two places. A row is a button that reads like a one-line card — the type glyph, the key at 500 `--ink2`, the summary ellipsised, the card's initials disc (empty for unassigned) and the raw status as a `neutral` `Pill` — and its click swaps THIS modal's issue, carrying the same snapshot forward: never a second modal over the first, because Escape has one owner and the walk is one dialog to the reader. No filter ring inside the tree.

**No controls in phase 2.** No `Assign to me`, `Move to…`, `Run ▾` or `Stop session`, and no `session` fact: the controls row is absent, not drawn disabled.

#### 8.9.3 Setup

Jira › Setup, `Setup.html` and `BoardsTen.html`: §8.6's Settings page applied to the boards this machine follows. A `Band` titled `Setup`, its sub-line naming what the page is and the file it writes (`~/.backlog-manager/settings/jira.json`), and in the right slot the `neutral` `this machine` pill — Settings › Shared's scope word, because everything on this page is written to the host. Below it, cards composed from `SettingsGroup` / `SettingsRow` (promoted to `components/ui/` for this) in Settings' own two hand-balanced columns, folding to one at Settings' 1100 px rather than at a third breakpoint of its own: Boards over Repositories on the left, the two cards a person edits; Connection on the right, the report (Skills joins it in phase 5). Rejected: boards as tiles with the query editor at the bottom of the page, and Setup as a rail entry of its own beside Jira.

**Boards.** A `New board` chip in the card's head. One row per board, sorted by name with `off` boards last (spec §1 overrides the mock's order): the name over `boardRowLine`'s sentence — `2 roots · 1 query · 14 issues · every 1m · polled 12 s ago`, zero counts omitted, an `off` board reading `sync off · last polled <age>` — with the last sweep's failure in `--red` after it, and two 28 px chips, `Open board` (the rail's own navigation threaded down, §8.0, landing on the Board with that board picked) and `Edit`. **The board sheet.** `JiraBoardSheet`, `BoardSheet.html` and `NewBoard.html`: `FormSheet`'s third composer (§8.7), one sheet for new and edit — titled `New board` under a `new board` kicker, interval `1m`, `Create board` and no delete for a new one; the board's own name under `edit board`, `Save` and `Delete board` for an edit. Three sections on hairline rules, each a 15/500 title over a 13 px `--ink2` line saying what it is for:

- **Name & sync** — two `SettingsRow`s, the name in the 36 px text field and the interval as a `Segmented` `pill` over `SYNC_INTERVALS`, the control the Trackers card draws for a GitHub repo's interval, so the two read alike.

- **Queries** — the four chips `+ Assigned to me`, `+ Open in current sprint`, `+ Unresolved in a project…` (which reveals a project `Select` in place) and `+ Custom JQL`, and one row per query: the label, the JQL in a 13 px textarea, and the check line under it — `4 issues match · checked 3 s ago` in `--green`, or Jira's parse message in `--red` with the textarea's stroke `--red` too — checked 500 ms after the last keystroke.

Mustard, not red: an unmapped project is a step not taken yet, not a failure. A refusal is shown under the add row in the server's words and changes nothing locally — the card only ever draws what the status says is stored.

**Connection.** A report of the server's `.env` and nothing to set: `Site`, `Signed in as` (the name Jira gives, never an e-mail; in `--red` with the reason while access is not `ok`), `Token` (`classic` or `scoped`, whose hint names the three scopes a scoped token needs and does not claim to have checked them — spec §1), and `Last sweep` (`every 15 s` or `idle`, `· N calls`, then `· last 429 <age> ago` in `--red` inside 15 min or `· no 429 since start`), closing on `restart the server to change it`, because a control here could only lie.

#### 8.9.4 Chip and Trackers row

**The chip.** `JiraChip`, `Trackers.html`: §8.0's tracker chip a second time, on the same `.tracker-chip*` classes — one look, because a second family for the same pill is how two chips come to disagree — drawn after it in the strip, and only while Jira is configured: a 26 px avatar with ONE initial (`?` while Jira has not said who the token belongs to — two letters are the card's reading), a red pip whenever access is not `ok` (no amber state: without credentials there is no chip), `Jira · <first name>` at 12.5/600, and two `Meter`s. `POLL` counts down to the soonest syncing board's next sweep on that board's own interval — `—` with no boards, amber `sync off` when every board is off, `…` before the first sweep, a full amber `overdue` after two silent intervals. `CALLS` is the last tick's spend against `JIRA_SWEEP_CALL_CEILING` (100, the app's yardstick for a lot of calls in one sweep, not Atlassian's limit), green, and red for 15 min after Jira's last 429 (`JIRA_429_RED_MS`) — a window, not a latch. The accessible name is `Jira: <full name>`; the meters ride as its description, as `TrackerChip`'s do (#230).

Its popover is `Popover` at 420 px, a reading with no links and no buttons (the rail rule): the identity over the site, the access phrase in `--red` with its detail when access is not `ok`, one wrapping 13 px line per board — `<name> · polled 12 s ago` and its failure in `--red`, in one column, because a board has no timer of its own to draw and a failure's sentence is the thing the row exists to show — and the sweep's two lines in the tracker popover's API block, worded by the Connection card's own `sweepLine` and `accessPhrase` so the two surfaces cannot word one fact two ways.

Below 700 px the chip moves into the rail bar beside the tracker's, one element in one home as §8.0 says. When both are there, neither draws its meters (§8.0): avatar, pip and caret only, so ☰ keeps its 36 px; a lone Jira chip keeps its meters.

**The Trackers row.** Settings › Shared › Trackers (§8.6) closes on one read-only Jira row while Jira is configured — `acme.atlassian.net · 2 boards · intervals live on Jira › Setup` — and carries no control: a board's interval is set on Setup's Boards card beside the roots it polls, and a second picker here would be a second writer of one setting. `Jira › Setup` is text, not a link, for the rail rule; the row is drawn whatever state the GitHub half is in, and not at all before Jira has answered or while it is unconfigured.
